Which numbers this answer rests on

Trusted: acceptance rate — read per source, never pooled — and user rating. Flagged as unreliable: median confidence, which rose every day of the week including the worst one, and average minutes saved, which barely varies and is self-estimated. Flag rate sits in between: useful as a tripwire, not as a quality score. Section 2 has the full ranking, section 3 the evidence.

4  Rank by source, not by workflow

This is Simpson's Paradox at work. Lead summary has a higher acceptance rate than Reply draft on its automatic source (65.8% via email, against 63.4% via queue). It also has a higher rate on the manual source (49.3% against 48.8%). It wins both. Yet combine each workflow's two sources into a single number and Lead summary comes out behind — 60.7% against 61.0%.

The reason is the mix, not the performance. Manual work is accepted far less often than automatic work in every workflow here. Lead summary takes 31% of its sessions manually; Reply draft takes only 16%. So Lead summary carries more of the harder kind of work, and its combined number gets pulled down further — far enough to wipe out a lead it genuinely holds on both sources. Pooling the sources ranks workflows by the kind of input they receive, not by how well they perform. That's why every rate in this report is read per source.

The most actionable finding: manual input costs ~13 points

Automatic sources average 61% acceptance; manual averages 48%. This holds across three unrelated workflows and three different teams, which points at the input path rather than any one prompt — and that may be fixable in the intake form rather than the model.

Caveat before acting: this data can't distinguish typing it in produces worse context from people only type it in when the case is unusual. Those need different fixes. Look at what actually gets pasted into that box.

5  What to investigate before rolling out further

Priority: what happened to Reply draft on 2026-08-07

Three explanations fit the same numbers, and they imply opposite actions:

  • Output got worse — then the rollout should pause.
  • The review gate got stricter — then output is unchanged and this is the policy working as designed.
  • It's composition — two manual rows are missing that day, and sessions more than halved.

Daily aggregates cannot separate these. There is no session-level key, so acceptance and flagging can't be joined per output.

Cheapest way to settle it: pull ~20 flagged Reply draft outputs from before the change and ~20 after, and have a team lead rate them blind. An afternoon's work, and it answers what the logs structurally can't. Separately, ask whoever owns the export why those rows are missing.

Not yet answerable: did the prompt change help?

No step change appears in any workflow at the prompt-change date. Feedback clustering was already drifting down beforehand; Lead summary is flat; Reply draft declines steadily throughout. The post-change window is three clean days, on rising volume, ending in a day that's both partial and confounded by the policy change.

Volume also grows daily, so pooled totals are weighted toward the post-change period — any “overall” number is quietly a post-change number. Give it two clean weeks with no other changes, then ask again.

The weekly check

Re-run this script on the new export. It is the check:

  • Section 3 — did anything get excluded, and are any days incomplete?
  • Section 4 — acceptance per workflow per source, never pooled.
  • Any workflow whose no-verdict share is climbing.
  • One line recording any prompt or policy change that week, so next week's reader knows what to attribute movement to.

Two things worth instrumenting so future weeks can answer more: a session-level key (so acceptance and flagging can be joined per output), and a reason code on flags (so “stricter policy” and “worse output” stop being indistinguishable).

Every rate here is a pooled count — numerator and denominator summed, divided once. Averaging per-row rates instead would over-weight small rows and, on this export, reverses the workflow ranking outright.
